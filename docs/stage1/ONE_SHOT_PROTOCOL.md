## One-shot message rule
N countries, N resident firms. Players H={G_i,F_i}. Feasible finite menu Z of fully specified standards contracts z=(participating governments, participating firms, technology standard/depth, IP rights, royalty terms, transfers). Contract z explicitly lists signature set P(z), all actors who must grant public recognition, essential private technological consent, or implementation commitments. In current bilateral benchmark P(z)={G_i,F_i:i in country-coalition}. A state cannot grant IP rights; nonmembers lacking relevant essential rights cannot veto.

Every actor simultaneously submits ONE fully identified z in Z or SQ. z activates iff **all** actors of P(z) submit EXACTLY z. No first proposer, chair priority, auction, numeric rank, or random tie-breaker. Since one actor can sign only one contract, two overlapping contracts can never both activate. Agreements on DISJOINT signature sets may both activate if technologically compatible; in three countries bilateral territorial consortium agreements necessarily overlap. If no contract activates, the national separate-standards default operates and firms still compete. A nonmember submitting a rival announcement does not veto a fully matched disjoint deal. Mixed private/public coalitions and signatures outside countries are feasible future menus but require proof of actual rights and model re-solves.

This is a simultaneous one-round *matching/consent game* — the market continuation is sequential, but no second bargaining round exists. Contract signing commits actors to implementation for this finite benchmark; voluntary *post-signature* adoption is a separate unimplemented game and cannot be presumed. Continuous royalties and complex partially overlapping multi-homing require further protocol and existence analysis. The finite menu assumption secures exact normal form existence, not novelty.

## Existence and binding constraints
Let V_h(z) denote appropriate downstream continuation payoff, V_h(SQ) default. For a single fully matched contract z, with outsider actors choosing SQ, a NECESSARY and SUFFICIENT **pure Nash** condition under this restricted mechanism is V_h(z)>=V_h(SQ) for every h in P(z) (each can unilaterally void z; nobody can singly create another agreement). Contract acceptance requires separate government and firm IR; no averaging government and firm payoffs because they are distinct players.

Every actor choosing SQ is ALWAYS a pure Nash announcement profile in this restricted menu (each P(z) has at least two required signatures, so one unilateral change activates nothing). Hence finite game pure NE always exists here; mixed Nash also exists by the finite-game existence theorem. Neither statement establishes UNIQUE standard choice or formation. It is a serious coordination-trap weakness of unanimity.

For a joint deviation from all-SQ to a specific z, if ALL required signers strictly prefer z, then all-SQ is **strong-Nash blocked** by P(z). A single accepted z is strong-Nash blocked if another feasible coalition can coordinate an alternative and make each deviator strictly better; assess outsiders' induced market losses correctly. The strong-deviation screen is NOT Bernheim–Peleg–Whinston coalition-proof Nash (requires recursive self-enforcement). General strong-Nash/CPNE existence is UNPROVED and can fail; no unrestricted standard selection theorem is claimed.
