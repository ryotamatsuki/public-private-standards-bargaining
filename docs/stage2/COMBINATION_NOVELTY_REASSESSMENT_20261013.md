## Important mathematical concern: mechanism conflation and platform dominance
Current verified LQ demand is p_i=a-(1-v)q_i-(gamma0+kappa*s-v*I)q_j in symmetric 2-firm examples. With m=1-v>0, b=gamma0+kappa*s-v*I and equal real c, the Cournot continuation is q=(a-c)/(2m+b), pi=m q², CS=(m+b)q². Comparing interface (s=0,I=1) and deeper platform (s=1,I=1) holds network compatibility fixed and increases b by kappa>0. For b>=0, this lowers per-firm operating profit and CS (d pi/db = -2m(a-c)^2/(2m+b)^3 <0; dCS/db= -b(a-c)^2/(2m+b)^3 <=0). Thus **if deeper platforms have no extra cost savings, outsider-distinctiveness gain, or network-density gain, they are dominated in the symmetric two-firm comparison**. A genuine Ghosh–Morita economic tradeoff generally needs an OFFSETTING ECONOMIC BENEFIT for deeper collaboration, not merely an increased substitutability parameter to manufacture comparative statics.

Comparing no shared standard vs joint deep platform changes cross coefficient by kappa-v, which collapses network and similarity changes into a NET TERM in this simple example. It cannot by itself prove a new nonseparable strategic mechanism. 3-firm outsider reallocation, country-incidence, separate consent rights and license cost distortions may break this simplicity, but NO SUCH THEOREM IS PROVED.
