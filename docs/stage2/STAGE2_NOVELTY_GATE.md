## 1. Actual audited starting object
N states and N firms, simultaneous, exclusive **exact-named-contract** announcements. A contract activates iff ALL legally essential public recognition / private licensing signatories submit the EXACT contract. Disjoint contracts can coexist, incompatible/overlapping proposals lack arbitrary priority. Upon agreement, state-dependent LQ differentiated-Cournot is independently re-solved for each national market; M_ii=1-v including singleton, M_ij=gamma0+kappa*s_ij-v*I_ij. Governments maximize domestic CS + resident firm's worldwide net profit; firms maximize own worldwide profit. Real standard costs vs private royalties must be separately accounted. The present verified model is finite-menu, binding implementation, no full production adoption stage, with multiple Nash profiles.

The candidate's core is **not** 'simultaneous proposals', 'strong Nash', 'network effects', 'Cournot after coalition formation', or 'products becoming similar.' All are known components. Stage 1 found 81 action profiles and 65 Nash profiles in an illustrative 2-state/2-firm/2-contract setting; this is a finite example, not a new theorem or prediction.

## 4. Whole-game, not title-match, absorption analysis
Application-neutral canonical object: finite strategic-form agreement-formation game with players h∈H=G∪F; action a_h∈{SQ}∪Z_h; outcome map \Omega(a)={z: a_h=z ∀h∈P(z)} subject to signatory-exclusivity/physical compatibility; continuation payoffs u_h(a)=W_h(\Omega(a)) for governments, \Pi_h(\Omega(a)) for firms, from downstream differentiated-Cournot equilibrium.

As an **abstract game**, this is a SPECIAL CASE of a finite normal-form consent/matching game; the no-contract Nash result is immediate and fully absorbed. Hart–Kurz models endogenous coalition announcements and strong stability, but it does not assert the present *standards-specific* sign/threshold result. There is currently NO such distinctive theorem to absorb, so a claim of full-game novelty is PREMATURE. Conversely it is not justified to say that one prior paper prints the *economically relevant* state/firm pair with differentiated markets and government-specific CS, technology licensing, and regulatory signatures: no such same-paper mapping was verified in the bounded search. **Absence of demonstrated mapping is NOT evidence that no such prior exists.**

Ray–Vohra and Economides–Skrzypacz use sequential proposer-driven formation (not our simultaneous exact consent) and partition-based transferable coalition worth, rather than the current mixed government/firm signatures. Difference in protocol BY ITSELF cannot count as new economics. Economides' second-stage 'oligopoly' payoff is mostly reduced-form (eq. 2, section 2.1), though it embeds compatibility/competition effects; our microfounded Cournot provides richer accounting, but is NOT by itself a theorem-level novelty claim.

Maggi–Ossa endogenizes country agreements but uses perfect-competition setting and producer lobbying, NOT stand-alone participating IP-owning firms choosing contracts. Fiedler endogenizes SSO innovators/implementers and governance, but no separately sovereign national regulatory approval players are documented at abstract-access depth. Casella's private cross-border coalition has a public/private juxtaposition; read its full model before claiming a broad mixed-authority first.

**Preliminary outcome:** no identified single **accessed** model exactly reproduces the economic joint-rights setup, but its complete strategy-space structure is well inside standard finite coalition games. Novelty can be earned ONLY from a NEW formal comparative-static/equilibrium/institutional consequence of two-key rights and heterogeneous W vs Pi, surviving exact generic absorption.

## 5. Nested-benchmark map and indispensable joint margin
B0: suppress public approval and G-player rights → Economides/Casella/Lukach-style private standards association benchmark (NOT exact demand-function recovery). B1: suppress indispensable privately licensed technology and F-player consent → Maggi/Ossa-style country regulation benchmark (NOT perfect-competition equivalence). B2: set v=kappa=0 → pure contract-rights bargaining over ordinary differentiated-Cournot payoffs; if two-key claim persists mechanically here, it may be ordinary consent complementarity and fail novelty. B3: align W_i's ordering of contracts with Pi_i's for all proposals → government participation is redundant when fully legally approved; a core two-key result should disappear in that restriction. B4: remove cross-border firm-profit incidence or inter-market Cournot competition → test whether cross-state strategic externality is indispensable. B5: remove licensing/payment price distortion while keeping real platform costs → distinguish transfer distribution from strategic rival-cost effects.

These are Stage-3/4 FUTURE diagnostics, NOT results established during Stage 2. There may be no parameter domain where B3 holds except an explicitly characterized one.

## 6. Novelty kill and strongest objections
1. "This is Hart–Kurz Γ with six named agents and an arbitrary payoff table." This is true for the **formation-game architecture and equilibrium existence**. Only an economically meaningful new theorem in the derived payoff family could survive.
2. "The novelty is that two actors both need consent." This is ordinary complementary-rights contracting; without a NONTRIVIAL alignment/misalignment threshold or coalition/institution welfare difference, KILL.
3. "Differentiation plus network benefits gives a competition tradeoff." Economides–Skrzypacz and Lukach–Kort–Plasmans already address it; KILL if no public/private feedback.
4. "Intervention changes standards and welfare." Maggi–Ossa/Mrázová and Gandal–Shy already address country decisions; KILL if public role is a decorative veto.
5. "A more flexible joint contract helps everyone." Merely enlarging feasible set/side transfers is not a bargaining result; compare same feasible terms and rights.
6. "Our equilibrium is the mutually preferred standard." Unanimous exact matching permits many no-deal Nash equilibria; any unique selected outcome needs an explicit, defended refinement AND existence proof.
7. "The standard is proprietary so any private firm may veto." Need indispensable IP rights AND government legal recognition; a voluntary standard not requiring public authorization cannot use an invented veto.
