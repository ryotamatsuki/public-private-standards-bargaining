## 5. Editor-facing 153-word contribution case (diagnostic; why NOT paper-ready)
A theory of international technology standards should explain when markets fragment into regional standards, when full international harmonization emerges, and how government recognition rights interact with firms’ incentives to share technologies. The constructed model provides a tractable differentiated-Cournot account of compatibility gains, increased insider substitutability, weaker substitution against outsiders, technology costs, and national consumer and producer welfare. Its two exact examples establish a regional outcome at weaker network intensity and a universal standard at stronger intensity, with a government–firm disagreement over optimal technical depth. These are valid analytical calculations, but the standards-size transition is anticipated by existing standards-coalition models, whereas the endogenous cooperation-depth tradeoff and outsider-differentiation effect are each established in closely related work. In both verified regimes, an otherwise identical firm-only or government-only bargaining institution selects the SAME standard structure as joint public/private consent. The remaining depth interval follows familiar Pareto bargaining logic. We therefore lack a distinct economic prediction requiring the full institutional architecture and cannot yet offer a convincing journal-level contribution claim.
