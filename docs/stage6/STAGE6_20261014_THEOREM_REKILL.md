## 3. Fundamental test: does the reported outcome require the model's economic architecture?
We construct an independent abstraction containing ZERO firms' product-market quantities, ZERO network externalities, ZERO product substitution and ZERO Cournot matrices, but with exactly the SAME 6-agent RS/IS contract-signature protocol. For every normalized quadratic-depth fee F>=7/2, choose
   P_N=0, W_N=1; P_R(s)=5s−Fs², W_R(s)=1+6s−Fs²;
   P_I(s)=4s−Fs², W_I(s)=1+7s−Fs²;
   P_O(s)=0, U_O(s)=1.
A consistent *abstract* govt firm+CS accounting is CS_R=1+s, CS_I=1+3s (both strictly positive); NO demand microfoundation is asserted. Exact squares give
  s_R=5/(2F)=argmax P_R; s_I=7/(2F)=argmax W_I;
  P_R(s_R)−max_t P_I(t)=9/(4F)>0;
  W_I(s_I)−max_t W_R(t)=13/(4F)>0;
  W_R(s_R)−W_N=35/(4F)>0;
  P_I(s_I)−P_N=7/(4F)>0.
Therefore BOTH RS(s_R) and IS(s_I) are strong Nash of the REDUCED Stage1 proposal game for EVERY finite F>=7/2. RS is robust to every outsider preannounced IS because the incumbent firm's veto is strict against every t. Both depths are O(1/F) as F→infinity. Firm-only selection = RS type (because P_R maximum > P_I maximum); government-only selection = IS type (because W_I maximum > W_R maximum), joint consent = BOTH.
This is an exact continuous-depth PROOF, not numerical sampling, and a general-multiple-player non-market COUNTERMODEL. See **code/verify_stage6_generic_veto_absorption.py** (SymPy identities, three exact F regressions, CI).

**Logical consequence:** *Merely* establishing (a) same-depth firm RS preference vs government IS preference, (b) joint RS/IS strong-stability coexistence, (c) private-only RS vs government-only IS, and (d) robust high-cost partial-depth persistence with s~1/F cannot be claimed as new findings requiring NETWORK GOODS or ENDOGENOUS DIFFERENTIATION; all four follow from an abstract quadratic-fee consent game with appropriately different payoff slopes. In a pair of generic contract classes X∈{R,I} with gross payoffs to agents P_X^0(s)=P_N+a_PX*s+O(s²), W_X^0(s)=W_N+a_WX*s+O(s²), and common quadratic fixed cost −F*s², the four strict slope conditions

## 5. Critical institutional/market identification risks
1. Exogenous v affects BOTH diagonal own-network value m=1−v and compatibility shift −v*s. Therefore varying v is NOT an isolated change to cross-network externalities; causal wording must reflect both channels.
2. s alone simultaneously controls true interoperability, product commonality and cost savings. This is a reduced-form **tie** between three economically distinct choices, not endogenous geographic product location or Ghosh–Morita's exact microfoundation. Optional two-control PR#11 exists separately but must NOT be silently mixed into this single-control source model.
3. Firm veto vs govt recognition rights were exogenously *assumed* as necessary signatures; no credibility/sanction/licensing microfoundation. Generic public/private holdout results do not become novel merely because the agents are relabeled governments and firms.
4. Strong first-stage contract Nash conditional on noncooperative Stage2 Cournot is an explicit HYBRID equilibrium selection, not a subgame-perfect strong equilibrium of the whole extensive form (Cournot bilateral reductions can benefit both firms). Strategic institutional timing, ability to implement coalition deviations, renegotiation protocols matter.
5. Even among standards literature, negotiated consent dynamics / the role of producer lobbying/public interests are treated by Farrell–Simcoe 2012, Gandal–Shy 2001 and Maggi–Ossa 2023. The current exact-match one-shot protocol has the SQ pure SPNE for any θ: its stronger selection is assumption-sensitive.
6. An abstract generic utility function can replicate any pure payoff ranking by construction. To make a specific new IO contribution, derive a **DEMAND/TECHNOLOGY-BASED restriction on feasible parameter-space geometry** that predicts previously unknown comparative static signs/bounds/welfare reversals, or implement an economically grounded joint-public/private institution producing a non-generic divergence.
