## 1. Why three sovereign governments matter more than two

Take the TRUE previously certified Stage15 privately founded O(1,a*(eta)),O(1,a*(eta)),N bilateral RS history in the reduced N/O and L/M game, with (v,gamma0,kappa,rho,phi)=(.01,.14,.02,.32,.14), eta∈[.10,.14], K∈[.54,.56], private common-design optimum a*(eta)>0 unique and increasing, a*(eta)∈(.3053,.61). Both incumbent governments G1,G2 prefer public M=O(1,0) to retaining private RS; outsider G3 prefers RS and vetoes M. The proposer G1's preference for **actual enacted intervention** is STRICT, but because G3 vetoes, G1's mere doomed proposal M is a weak best response and choosing L also is; NEVER misstate strictly positive incentive to submit a doomed bill.

Under a public mandate, companies' conform-or-exit game has unique all-three-compliance Nash. W_i accounts for their REAL up-front sunk capital stock and the eventual public interface. Exact per-national normalized BEFORE-transfer domestic W changes from RS to IS M after sunk RS resources:

`B(eta,a)=Delta W_G1=Delta W_G2=W_I0-(phi/2)a²-[f_R(eta,a)+CS_R(eta,a)]>0`

`-L(eta,a,K)=Delta W_G3=W_I0-K-[P_out(eta,a)+CS_R(eta,a)]<0`

with `W_I0` net of phi/2 implementation capital but before K, f_R=3m x²-phi(1+a²)/2, P_out=3m y², m=1-v, private RS Cournot x,y unchanged. All signs are exact full-rectangle Stage14 Bernstein certificates.

With cash-like *non-distortionary, enforceable, internally balanced* intergovernmental lump-sum transfers, either government 1 can offer `t_1>=0` and government2 `t_2>=0`, to be paid to G3 **ONLY IF M is approved and implemented**. Then G1,G2 national ex-post gain is `B-t_i`, G3's gain `-L+t1+t2`. Every government is national welfare maximizer. This additional facility is NOT asserted to exist in actual WTO or other international institutions; the fiscal incidence and enforceability are explicit new assumptions.

From Stage11 strict private concavity, cross-derivative and rational isolating optimum at eta=.10, and positive da*/deta, `a*(eta) in (.3053,.61)` for every eta in [.10,.14].

Define the one-donor shortfall `H=L-B`. The worst case for H across K∈[.54,.56] occurs at K=.54. Exact rational tensor Bernstein positivity of both numerator and denominator after mapping the ENTIRE real rectangle
`eta=.10+.04 T,`
`a=.3053+.3047 U`, `T,U∈[0,1]`

Thus the proposer G1 cannot privately fund an outsider's economically rational YES vote without sacrificing more own W than it gains, even though G1 STRONGLY prefers implementing M before the transfer cost. This is a SHARP institutional distinction from aggregate beneficial harmonization.

## 3. Proposition 16B: joint financing possible exactly when true ex-post aggregate surplus is positive

The two RS member countries together can make G3 weakly whole with nonnegative balanced real transfers and **each** be weakly better off exactly when `2B>=L`; strict donor gains are possible iff `2B>L`.

By direct algebra with true sunk pre-existing two-firm capital, `2B-L = K_post(eta)-K` where
`K_post(eta)=3W_I0-[2f_R(a*,eta)+P_out(a*,eta)+3CS_R(a*,eta)]-phi(a*)²`.
This is the Stage15 **actual AFTER-investment** public-switch welfare threshold, not Stage11's comparison of two separately financed NEW SPE paths `K_world(eta)`.

Previous exact rational Bernstein certificates prove `2B-L` strictly decreases with eta after substituting the unique increasing a*(eta). With exact endpoint brackets for a*(eta=.10)∈(.3053,.3054) and a*(eta=.14)∈(.5949,.5950), `2B-L>0` at eta=.10 even at K=.56, and `2B-L<0` at eta=.14 even at K=.54. Therefore:

**THEOREM 16B**: For EVERY real K in the full closed [.54,.56], there is a UNIQUE `eta_T(K)∈(.10,.14)` such that real nationally welfare-improving, BUDGET-BALANCED G1/G2 financing is possible if eta<eta_T, and impossible if eta>eta_T. eta_T strictly decreases in K.

Numerically, eta_T(.54)≈.114413357802984, eta_T(.55)≈.111240512597226, eta_T(.56)≈.107833490653772; these are numerical roots of an exact analytically one-crossing function, not approximate proofs.

The distinction between individual ability `B>=L` and coalition ability `2B>=L` is essential to this **THREE-GOVERNMENT** argument: G1 alone CANNOT buy a veto ANYWHERE in the chosen region, whereas G1+G2 CAN in a proper subregion.

## 4. Proposition 16C: even efficient side payments need not happen — simultaneous voluntary contribution coordination failure

AFTER the RS investment is sunk and G1 indicates it seeks public M, let G1,G2 simultaneously and publicly choose nonnegative **binding conditional contribution pledges** `t1,t2` payable only if the mandate wins unanimous votes; G3 accepts if `t1+t2 >= L` (the equal-utility YES tie is EXPLICITLY part of the institution), and G1/G2 approve if `B-t_i>=0`. Without approval all pledged transfers are cancelled, not wasted.

In the entire eta-K rectangle, `L>B>0` from Proposition 16A. This gives an EXACT zero-pledge pure-strategy Nash equilibrium `t1=t2=0` with the original G3 veto: either single donor would have to raise a pledge to at least L while it can afford at most B; doing so strictly reduces its own W. The familiar threshold-collective-action free-rider equilibrium is therefore real and remains present even when M increases world W.

If `eta<eta_T(K)`, `2B>L>B`; for ANY
`t1∈(L-B,B), t2=L-t1`,
the treaty passes, each member's net gain `B-t_i>0`, and G3 is exactly made whole. At the exact contribution threshold a donor cannot reduce without making G3 veto, and cannot increase without lowering its own payoff, so EACH such profile is ALSO a pure Nash equilibrium. It is a continuum of cost-sharing allocations. At equality `2B=L`, both donors can be weakly indifferent; at eta>eta_T, the full contract is impossible as a Pareto improvement.

At eta=.10, K=.55, `a*=.30534434`, `B=.0495227585`, `L=.0686593738`. Contributions `t1=t2=.0343296869` leave each member strict national gain ≈.0151930715 and G3 exactly indifferent. Yet the zero-pledge veto is also a Nash equilibrium: unilateral donor must promise .068659 > .049523 benefit.

## 5. Proposition 16D: a PAYING treaty continuation CANNOT be anticipated along the SAME full private-RS investment path

A crucial separate backward-induction check prevents a serious overclaim. Suppose the firms can foresee that at `h_R=(O(1,a*),O(1,a*),N)` the government treaty DONORS select one of the successfully funded unanimity equilibria and mandate O(1,0). Then an original private regional founder who had already spent on extra positive design earns net public standard profit
`P_I^0-K-phi*(a*)²/2`.
Instead it can UNILATERALLY change its first-stage investment to N. The new private history becomes `(N,O(1,a*),N)`, where **all three governments strictly prefer the public mandate even with zero financing** (`W_I0-K>W_N` for both N-government countries, and `W_I0-W_N+phi/2>0` for the prior O-firm government). Thus public M still passes and the deviating founder earns `P_I^0-K`. The strict first-stage gain is exactly
`phi*(a*)²/2>0`.

Consequently: **a funded-M continuation at h_R is an economically valid government SUBGAME equilibrium, but it cannot sit on a full six-player SPE path with those same original firms simultaneously investing O(1,a*),O(1,a*),N**. Such ex-post policy deals change ex-ante private investment incentives; readers should not label an efficient compensation equilibrium an additional full-game SPE outcome without resolving first-stage investment. An unfunded G3-veto continuation preserves the baseline Stage15 ONPATH first-stage RS incentives, but the FULL treaty-augmented all-history SPE correspondence still requires completing each offpath financing subgame.

The intertemporal lesson: a prospective international compensation treaty may destroy the ex ante private platform investment that created the need for the treaty. This hold-up observation is mechanically similar to known dynamic bargaining/installed-base games; no independent novelty GO.

## 6. Why this strengthens the case for three countries, and how to present it

- A **third sovereign outsider** simultaneously (i) benefits from remaining differentiated and vetoes a public standard, and (ii) demands transfer L to concede.
- An **agenda-setting RS member** strictly prefers executed regulation, but its local gain B is insufficient to buy approval on its own.
- The **second RS member** is not redundant: it supplies precisely the possible additional share B that changes funding feasibility.
- The national transfer coalition 2B vs L and the ex-post aggregate welfare test are equivalent under costless lump-sum transfers; **whether the transfer gets organized** is a DIFFERENT Nash equilibrium issue.
- At high eta the outsider's refusal coincides with a true negative world surplus; thus majority overrule or compensation at any price is NOT uniformly efficient.
- This is NOT evidence that an arbitrary two-country model cannot generate standardization; it explains why 3 distinct country payoffs and voting rights are necessary for **this particular endogenous 2-donor-versus-one-holdout** policy mechanism.

For a main-paper MINIMAL presentation, Stage15's 3-firm/3-government open O/N + no-transfer M/L game remains the core. A **brief Proposition/Corollary about compensatory financing** may be valuable, but the voluntary contribution equilibrium set and full treaty-enhanced initial-firm SPE should appear as a tightly marked extension, not be imported into the baseline without re-solving.

**Related prior art / no superficial originality claim:** Takarada et al. (2020), _Standards Policy and International Trade: Multilateralism versus Regionalism_ already model national-outsider harmonization blocking under core: https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.12425 ; literature on side payments, transfer credible commitments and international negotiation includes Kapstein (2025), _Side Payments in World Politics: Theory and Practice_, https://doi.org/10.1111/1758-5899.70022, and _Vote-trading in International Institutions_ (2008), European Journal of International Law https://academic.oup.com/ejil/article/19/1/3/430795 .
