# Stage 17 — Full-history SPE with government transfer pledges; unanimity vs majority under independent continuous private design

**Date** 2026-10-14. **Status** Research proposition for the **reduced six-player game** (3 independent firms, each chooses either N or O(c,a) with continuum 0<=a<=c<=1; national governments G1 agenda, G2 vote, G3 outsider vote; public mandate M=existing public-domain O(1,0), or L nonintervention). We ADD sequential institutional choices: after observing initial firm technologies, G1 and G2 simultaneously and publicly pledge binding, nonnegative lump-sum payments t1,t2 to G3, **payable ONLY IF M passes** and refunded otherwise. After pledges, G1 proposes M/L, G2 and G3 vote as required by unanimous or simple majority rule, and the firms independently choose comply/exit if M passes. National treasury transfers do not directly subsidize or tax firms and have zero real collection/transaction cost. Every government chooses national W including transfers; vote and proposal indifference favors M/YES. Same-Cournot product payoffs, intertechnology installed-capital stock and c/a real costs as in Stage15. This added facility is an ASSUMED institutional right, not a claim it exists in actual standards treaties.

The original five market coefficients are
`(v,gamma0,kappa,rho,phi)=(.01,.14,.02,.32,.14)`. Outsider distinctiveness `eta∈[.10,.14]`; additional real family setup K∈[.54,.56]. Demand SPD and Cournot support, privately optimized interior a_R*(eta)∈(.3053,.61), public-adoption *strictly dominant* all-3 conformity and all private global initial-deviation bounds are inherited from exact independent Stage11/14/15 certificates, explicitly reexecuted by the dedicated Stage17 CI.

## I. Government subgame lemma: an actual pure credible continuation for EVERY private-technology history

After a specific initial h, solve final market Cournot twice, with L vs fully complied M, including all real sunk investment and actual incremental retrofit costs. Let `δ_i(h)=W_i(M|h)-W_i(L|h)`, a REAL value for each government.

After conditional funding t1,t2≥0, G1's prospective gain is `δ1-t1`, G2's `δ2-t2`, G3's `δ3+t1+t2`. After pledges are observed G1 proposes M iff its gain is nonnegative AND sufficient other votes permit adoption, G2/G3 vote YES iff individual gain is nonnegative, with all ties resolved YES/M. Thus the funding-stage payoff is these national gains when M succeeds, otherwise exactly zero relative to L (rejected payments refunded).

**LEMMA 17.1 (pure equilibrium for all REAL delta triples, not grid only):**

UNANIMITY: If δ1<0 or δ2<0, no public M can pass without subsidizing G1/G2 (not allowed), so zero pledges and L are a Nash outcome. If both donor-government policy gains are nonnegative and δ3≥0, zero pledges and M pass. If δ1,δ2≥0 and δ3<0, let `L_3=-δ3>0`. If some donor j has gain ≥L_3, a transfer t_j=L_3 and t_other=0 is a pure Nash with M (and if gain=L_3 the donor is indifferent); if both donors have gain <L_3 but δ1+δ2≥L_3, choose t1=L_3 δ1/(δ1+δ2) and t2=L_3 δ2/(δ1+δ2), which meet the threshold exactly and leave each donor weakly better off. If neither individual can afford L_3, *zero pledges and rejection* are ALSO Nash, including when δ1+δ2>L_3 (no single donor can gain by unilaterally pledging the entire outsider loss). If δ1+δ2<L_3, zero pledges and no mandate is the only indicated financing outcome (the lemma does not claim to enumerate all government strategies under equality cases).

MAJORITY, treating the G1 proposer as its own YES vote: If δ1<0, zero pledges imply L. If δ1≥0 and either δ2≥0 or δ3≥0, zero pledges imply M is passed by G1 and at least one other government. If δ1≥0,δ2<0,δ3<0, only G1 can rationally finance G3 to secure the second YES. With L_3=-δ3: if δ1≥L_3, its exact pledge t1=L_3,t2=0 supports M; otherwise zero pledges and L form an equilibrium. Pledges cannot induce G2 to vote YES when δ2<0, since t2 is a nonnegative PAYMENT by G2, not a subsidy TO G2.

The above algebra gives a nonempty **pure government Nash/SPE strategy selection** after EVERY possible private firm history h in the entire first-stage uncountable continuum. Any donor pledge that funds M is PIVOTAL because its sum is exactly L_3. Lowering it causes G3 to veto, forfeiting its weakly positive M benefit; raising it strictly reduces donor W. At zero funding when the mandate is rejected, any unilateral contribution sufficient to cross the entire L_3 costs at least as much as the donor's own gain, so cannot strictly improve. Thus offpath government continuation existence is **proved**, not assumed by an inappropriate generic compactness argument about discontinuous pledge provision. An executable rational-grid adversarial unit check evaluates 17^3 * 2 donor-selection modes * 2 voting rules = **19,652 exact rational triple cases**; these samples SUPPORT but do not replace the all-real casewise lemma.

**Limits of lemma:** G1 and G2 are only donor governments, G3 only recipient. There is no possibility of G2 demanding payments when δ2<0 or G1 buying its own vote, and no other transfer channels. A richer general bilateral negotiation/treaty agenda would need new equilibrium analysis. If a nonmember with δ3≥0 is indifferent on a vote, a different tie-break rule could matter for funded thresholds.

## II. Full six-player initial firm SPE theorem under treaty + UNANIMITY

**Theorem 17.2:** For every eta∈[.10,.14], K∈[.54,.56], the FULL three-firm independent continuous N/O design first stage, followed by the pledge/proposal/unanimous votes and independent firm conformity/game, has BOTH:

- OPEN private RS: `h_R=(O(1,a_R*(eta)),O(1,a_R*(eta)),N)`. At this observed history G1/G2 privately gain B>0 each from actually enforced M, while G3 loses L>B from M, proven Stage14/16 by GLOBAL Bernstein signs. The *zero-pledge/NOMANDATE* donor equilibrium is thus CREDBILE. G1 weakly proposes an ultimately vetoed M (strict benefit from actual implementation but known rejection), G2 YES, G3 VETO; final policy L and product market RS with an active third independent producer. An equivalent agenda strategy simply chooses L. This distinction separates strict desire for intervention from a strict willingness to file a doomed proposal.
- PUBLIC IS: `h_I=(N,N,N)`. All three national δ_i=W_I0−K−W_N>0. No donor pledges needed, G1 M and G2/G3 approve, all independent firms STRICTLY comply, final IS(1,0).

For every corporate history h (not merely these two), Lemma 17.1 constructs a valid pure funding/proposal/voting continuation under national unanimity. The M adoption continuation is uniquely all complying for all N/O histories in the K rectangle, and final Cournot KKT is unique. Therefore the full real extensive game can be completed into subgame-perfect strategies rather than conditioning on undefined offpath political behavior.

**First-stage actual deviations** are blocked by Stage15 exact global all-continuous bounds, which are insensitive to WHICH credible L/M governmental result obtains after an arbitrarily deviating firm's history (treasury transfers go only between governments):
- An RS member changing to any O(c,a) cannot exceed its globally privately optimized regional profit under L; if M passes, physically installed old design plus retrofit resource costs cannot undercut direct public O(1,0), so net operating profit ≤public IS payoff `P_I0−K` < its RS payoff. Changing to N induces unanimously supported public M at the singleton-O history, gives it `P_I0−K` again, strictly less than original RS member payoff.
- The nonmember F3 changing from N to any O(c,a) has heterogeneous Cournot entry output strictly below 3/5 even if others stay O(1,a*), yielding after fixed costs <.5292 versus its actual outsider profit >.547893. If instead M passes, its net payoff is at most `P_I0−K` < its original outsider payoff.
- A firm initially N in the all-N IS history changing to arbitrary O(c,a) earns either an expensive standalone technology profit `P_N−K−variable-cost` under L, which is less than its IS payoff, or no more than its IS payoff under M with unrefundable capitalized O→O implementation.
- These cover EVERY real 0<=a<=c<=1 unilateral private deviation. The constitutional right to public O(1,0), institutional G1 agenda order, and local national payoff objective remain explicit primitives, not endogenous outcomes.

**No inference that funded government subgame Nash is also a financed-M six-player SPE at the SAME O-O-N firm history**: this is false, as shown next.

## III. Ex-post funded treaty cannot stay on the old O-O-N private initial SPE path

At h_R if 2B>L, a different credible conditional-pledge government Nash implements M with t1+t2=L. When firms foresee this M, each original privately committed O(1,a*) founder earns `P_I0−K−phi a*²/2`; unilateral deviation of one original founder to N leaves a singleton O case in which ALL THREE government δ positive and public M passes without any transfer. The deviating company earns `P_I0−K`, a strict difference `phi a*²/2>0`. This is a true initial-firm **counterexample** to simply labeling the financed-M treaty continuation an SPE with original privately invested founders. It does not rule out different private choices or different funded treaty timing rules generating other equilibria.

## IV. MAJORITY: a full continuous public IS SPE survives; positive-design bilateral profile excluded in a whole design interval

**Theorem 17.3:** Under simple 2-of-3 majority with the exact same firms/governments, public IS h_I=N,N,N retains full-continuous SPE status with zero transfer pledges and unanimous support at the all-N history. Any one firm O(c,a) deviation is globally bounded by either L singleton net payoff or the already attained M payoff, exactly as under unanimous voting. Credible pure offpath pledge/vote continuations exist at EVERY h from Lemma 17.1.

**Theorem 17.4 (strict exclusion of a WHOLE SET of majority initial candidate profiles):** For every eta∈[.10,.14], K∈[.54,.56] and even for every observed common-design level `a0∈[1/4,61/100]` (regardless of whether privately optimal), the symmetric bilateral private history
`(O(1,a0),O(1,a0),N)`
cannot be a pure initial-action SPE of the MAJORITY treaty game. Stage14 exact 2D Bernstein proves G1/G2 each strictly prefer public M at the observed h, so majority enacts M **without government contributions** (zero pledge is a Nash since the motion already passes). A founding firm changes initial O(1,a0) to N, producing a singleton open-hISTORY; then all 3 governments prefer and approve M, while the company saves strictly `phi*a0²/2 ≥phi*(1/4)²/2=7/1600=.004375` real stock expense. Its final production and Cournot payoff under M are otherwise identical. Therefore any positive a0 in this whole rectangle is unstable, not merely the exact private optimum a_R*. This is a strong *same-technology*, *same-market* voting-regime comparison.

**Not proved**: exhaustive initial pure/mixed SPE correspondence under simple majority, absence of all asymmetric regional standards or private standard deviations at c<1, global outcome uniqueness. A simple local no-RS exclusion is NOT logically the proposition that public IS is the only majority-rule SPE.

## V. Policy interpretation and economic novelty red team

- Unanimity makes the third government's veto **pivotal**, supports an otherwise unsustainable *privately built* positive common design RS, and leaves IS as an alternative coordination outcome.
- Conditional governmental compensation can be *ex-post feasible* yet fail through a pure Nash free-rider donor equilibrium, so simply adding a legal finance tool does not necessarily alter ex-ante privately invested RS. Under a funded continuation it generally changes private development before any approval, preventing naive offpath-to-SPE promotion.
- Majority eliminates the specific positive-common-design bilateral RS candidate and generates a strictly profitable **first-stage avoidance of sunk product-homogenization investment**, while allowing IS. The result is not purely a voting-result comparison.
- The historic Stage11 welfare frontier and Stage15/16 post-investment welfare/transfer frontier remain **DISTINCT**. Endogenous a_R*(eta) moves social and political thresholds, but coalition/veto/finance alone are already extensively studied.

**Important new closest literature:** Harstad (2007), *Harmonization and Side Payments in Political Cooperation*, **American Economic Review 97(3):871–889**, https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.97.3.871, already studies cooperative harmonization with and without side payments and institutional choice, including negotiation delay. The refunded-pledge / provision-point game and continuum of funded Nash are familiar from Bagnoli–Lipman (1989) and the review in https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272716301323 . Thus **not novel** to say "intergovernmental side payments can solve harmonization vetoes" or "contribution games have no-provision and provision Nash." Takarada et al (2020), Ghosh–Morita (2012), Gil Moltó et al (2005), Lukach–Kort–Plasmans (2007) are still direct prior-art threats. The candidate contribution that must survive those sources is the *joint* endogenous private common-product-design cutoff, the existence of separate country-veto-supported private/public six-player SPE, changes to INITIAL private investment induced by voting and financing, and the reversal in whether enforcing/compensating the blocked mandate raises real ex-post national/world welfare.

**Suggested manuscript order:** baseline minimal (3 firms × 3 national governments, O/N, L/M) first. State private-design threshold and RS/IS SPE existence (Stage11+Stage15), government votes plus majority deviation. Then a short funding Corollary distinguishing compensated-veto opportunities, pure contribution selection and preinvestment reversal (Stage16/17). Avoid sprawling standards games with no added predictions. Core article originality still NEEDS theorem-specific full-text literature audit.
