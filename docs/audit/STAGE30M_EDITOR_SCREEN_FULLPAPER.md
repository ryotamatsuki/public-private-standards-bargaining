## 2. How much independence between the two advertised results?

**Not two new primitive ideas:** compatible investment, sunk K, minimum technology, government consent and equilibrium welfare threshold are separately well established. Calling seven results seven novelties invites a justified rejection.

**Result A (C1+C3+C7) is the driving economics.** Actual content is the GLOBAL FIRST-STAGE equilibrium correspondence and the resulting political support reversal across different players—not mere arithmetic that two yes-voting governments satisfy a two-vote quorum. Outsider fresh-cost incidence K is the key endogenous constraint. If C1 were only a symmetric candidate, paper would be too weak; it is an iff over an entire continuum with global deviations.

**Result B (C2+C5+C6) is a derived but substantive extension.** The final iff \(K\le K_W(\eta)\) is a simple max-vs-constant comparison once C2 and C5 are available. Nonetheless obtaining the C5 maximum over ALL **SPE-supportable** two-stock private designs and showing real coexistence of world welfare above and below M at identical eta,K are nontrivial model implications. The conceptual contribution is a **welfare guarantee under unspecified pure equilibrium selection** and the best-/worst-case tradeoff, NOT the mere existence of a welfare threshold.

**Conclusion:** A+B belong in one paper as a **single joint mechanism with two complementary propositions**, which is a legitimate architecture; neither should be submitted as two separate papers, and B alone would likely be read as algebra/accounting.

## 4. Three mandatory nontrivial referee objections (in order)

### FATAL-to-general-claim A — Institutional policy stage is rule rather than fully strategic voting

Under r=2, unrestricted simultaneous NO,NO,NO is a nonpivotal Nash continuation even when each nation materially favors M. C3's all-M consequence and C6's pure-SPE constitutional comparison therefore do NOT extend to ordinary unrefined voting equilibria. If a paper calls its model "all strategic firm-government subgame-perfect equilibria" without qualifying the **prescribed sincere policy continuation**, it is WRONG. A fully journal-reviewable paper may study a *constitutional rule that mechanically implements a sincere motion/approval selector*, but then must explain why this rule—not strategic government tactics—is the object of research. No claim to empirically common institution; more robust political implementation would require a different model.

### FATAL-to-general-claim B — Conditional H shock at third O(0,0)

Original pair O(1,.5),O(1,.5),N at eta=.14 gives founder-outsider H_13=H_23=.07. Changing N to O(0,0), paying K but buying no incremental c/a, makes H_13=H_23=.14. A certification/status interpretation (K finances true baseline category functionality) makes the *reduced-form* binary choice logically coherent, but a specialist can question why losing the two-seller family distinction ERASES the entire cross-segment differentiation. C1's three-O exclusion depends on the precise three-O branch. It is not sufficient to claim the result is a generic standardization mechanism; do not fabricate a microfoundation. A journal can publish a transparent reduced-form benchmark, but this exact demand assumption lowers the result's transferability.

### FATAL-to-broad-generalization C — g=0 policy adoption and narrow parameter support

A real government's extra mandatory administration fee g=.04, holding other structure, already admits private L in earlier Stage30 negative diagnostic with r=2 and broad agenda: all-private-equilibrium elimination does not transport. Likewise the strongest theorems are certified for five fixed coefficients plus two intervals. This is a valid theorem in a clearly delimited class, but not yet a model-independent constitutional design result. The manuscript must avoid an over-general policy recommendation and connect the numerical parameter region to robust strict economic inequalities if it wants journal-level significance rather than a carefully chosen example.
