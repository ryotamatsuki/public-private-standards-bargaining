## 1. Explicit knife-edge comparison: N vs O(0,0)

Take two private founders each investing \`O(1,.5)\` and firm 3 choosing \`N\`. At \`gamma=.14, eta=.14, kappa=.02, v=.01\`:

- \`H12=gamma+kappa*.5-v*1=.14\`.
- \`H13=H23=gamma-eta*.5=.07\`, hence a bilateral founders' product-differentiation gap vs the external firm.
- \`z=(1.32,1.32,1)\`.

Change **only** firm 3's discrete initial choice from \`N\` to \`O(0,0)\`, paying setup \`K\` although buying zero continuous compatibility/commonality stock:

- All three firms now count as \`O\` developers. The original manuscript changes the private-demand **branch**.
- \`H12=.14\` remains, but \`H13=H23=gamma+kappa*min(.5,0)-v*min(1,0)=.14\`. These cross-effects EACH jump by +.07 even though entrant's quantitative stocks are zero.
- \`z=(1.32,1.32,1)\` remains exactly unchanged.
- Thus the discontinuity is precisely in founder–entrant substitution/relative differentiation, not incumbent demand intercept or effective common c; it is caused by the **binary O membership state**.

For general common founders O(c,a), the founder–outside cross-effect jumps from \`gamma-eta a\` (third N) to \`gamma\` (third O(0,0)), an increase of \`eta a\`. This jump is strictly positive for \`a>0,eta>0\` and is zero only at a=0. N and O(0,0) are **different discrete actions**: neither logical demand continuity nor economic equivalence is mandated by mathematics. But calling c,a “physical interoperable stocks” can make the jump economically unintuitive. The private triple-investor exclusion (C1) explicitly relies on the triple-demand branch and outsider entry envelope; therefore this is headline-relevant, not a minor descriptive note.

## 2. Candidate interpretations of the existing primitive — no new technology allowed

### I1. Registration or certification creates a categorical market affiliation
\`O(c,a)\` denotes an open-family **legal/market membership state** plus optional physical implementation stocks. Fixed real setup \`K\` funds basic recognized access/certification, even when \`c=a=0\`. An N firm is recognized by buyers as technologically *outside* the family. Once it chooses O(0,0), the **private-cohort exclusivity/differentiation** \`-eta a\` is no longer present, because all recognized producers compete within the same standard category. A zero-stock registered member has no demand intercept gain (\`z_3=1\`) and zero pairwise c with founders, but its product classification and resulting substitutability change.

- **Internal consistency:** This can **rationalize** a binary membership-dependent substitution effect while preserving all payoff primitives. It does NOT prove actual product interoperability at zero technical stock and is not a verified example from an ISO, EU or ITU system.
- **Objection:** If a is genuinely investment in product-level common technical functionality, third-firm affiliation with a=0 should not automatically eliminate the founders' differentiated service. Mere legal registration may not change cross-demand by exactly \`eta a\`. One needs an explicit buyer-recognition/brand umbrella or certification mechanism to justify the reduced form. A fully specified microfoundation would introduce demand-side assumptions and may require rollback.
- **Editorial status:** **POSSIBLE BUT NOT VALIDATED**; acceptable only for a deliberately discrete *market-family* benchmark, not for arbitrary physical interoperability standards. This is the only interpretive route to test before declaring baseline full-paper unsalvageable.

### I2. Pure technological compatibility / minimum capacity only
An O(0,0) entrant shares no compatibility with founders (minimum zero) and has no technical stock. If all product distances depended continuously only on the pair minima, an O(0,0) entrant would not remove the founders' external differentiated status. This intuition directly CONFLICTS with the model's sudden change in cross-effects. Under a pure capacity-only story the stated demand branch is not defensible. This alternative has **NOT BEEN SOLVED**; no claim about its equilibria is licensed.

### I3. Public standards council vote changes product demand before a mandate
False under the timing: the branch changes at firm 3's initial **private O** action, not at a government vote. Government rule cannot explain the zero-stock jump. **REJECT AS AN EXPLANATION**.

### Literature sanity check
Besen & Farrell (1994), *Choosing How to Compete: Strategies and Tactics in Standardization*, JEP 8(2),117–131, DOI https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.8.2.117 distinguishes competition **for** a standard vs **within** a standard and thus supports the **economic importance** of categorical competition modes. Farrell & Saloner (1986), *Installed Base and Compatibility*, AER 76,940–955, treats network compatibility and installed-base transitions. **Neither establishes** that an entrant with zero interoperable stock immediately changes pair-specific Cournot substitutions by \`eta a\`. The status-switch equation is our own reduced-form assumption and cannot be attributed to either source.
