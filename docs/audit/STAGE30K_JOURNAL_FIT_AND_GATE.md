## A. Why previous "near-zero novelty" implication was too harsh

Newness need NOT consist in inventing Cournot, minimum investment, majority rule or standards institutions. As:
- **Buccella et al. 2023 J Econ** explicitly adds a compatibility decision stage to established models and earns published contribution from equilibrium multiplicity, fixed-cost regimes, asymmetric equilibrium and welfare rankings;
- **Nabin et al. 2023 IJET** explicitly says several known modelling aspects are introduced together in one coherent framework, then obtains SSO-induced differences in firms' innovation and welfare;
- **Harstad 2005 QJE, Farrell & Simcoe 2012 RAND** obtain economic policy lessons from how constitutional/standards-setting rules alter *ex ante* incentives, not from inventing voting;
- **Takarada et al. 2020 JPET and Costinot 2008 JIE** distinguish different policy/legal systems by the ENDOGENOUS welfare and political equilibrium they support.

Our pure-SPE set theorem + welfare-support criterion can therefore count as two **substantive theorem results of a SINGLE integrated economic mechanism** if actual parent studies do not directly imply them. This is stronger than a technical extension but weaker than a general-purpose new industrial-organization theorem.

The central **distinct "who protects whose investment" fact** is opposite to what one might expect: firms constructing private standards cannot persuade their own governments to keep them, but an outsider state saves its own adoption K by blocking the public option. This strategic externality changes firms' prepolitical investment possibilities. Removing the veto through agenda eligibility may also destroy HIGH-welfare private equilibria. The chain is economically relevant, not just a rearranged voter tally.

## D. Strict falsification test for the marketed contribution

Consider a referee saying: "Nondiscounted outside option + minimum investment + government veto obviously produce R; the best/worst welfare comparison is tautological."

A credible rebuttal must cite a **derived and genuinely non-immediate result**, not extra model decoration:
- The complete **global** investor equilibrium set contains **only exactly two matching developers** with the outsider N even though 3 O profiles, off-path policy changes and any finite c/a deviations are admissible (C1).
- The private actors' home governments cannot be the political champions of the own privately beneficial standard anywhere in that entire set (C7).
- A constitution with dispersed proposal rights and an attainable majority makes private L entirely nonviable, but all public investments continue to have multiple profiles and same welfare (C2/C3).
- Old private pure SPE outcomes cross the public welfare level at the **same** eta,K, forcing the welfare risk frontier (C5/C6).

If the published older sources demonstrate this **entire chain** as a theorem in an adequately general parent model, the contribution collapses to an application or numerical example and the journal plan must change. If not, the completed *correspondence-plus-constitutional-welfare* unit is potentially original and independently interesting at journal level, even though the algebraic tail of C6 is simple.
