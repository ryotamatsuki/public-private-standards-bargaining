# Stage30K — Re-positioning the economic value of C1–C7

**Date** 2026-10-15 JST. **Purpose:** Correct Stage30J's excess discount of integrated extension work, without losing its valid source-priority and primitive fragility objections. These are editorial judgments, not a new proof, model, human referee report or published-priority certificate.

## 1. The central question should be a new economic question, not "a 3-country agenda theorem"

**Core research question:**
> Can firms' sunk compatibility investments create a privately sustainable regional standard that their own governments wish to replace, and when does expanding governments' ability to mandate a common public interface improve welfare **regardless of which private equilibrium was selected**?

This simultaneously tests the **political identity of the protector** and **the consequences of removing a continuum of privately supportable investments**. It is unavailable in a model featuring *only* private compatibility investments or *only* states negotiating exogenous mandatory standards, because neither alone contains the full divergence of private firm objectives and later government choice.

The economic mechanism is **outsider-protected private coordination**:
1. Two private investors share a bilateral minimum compatibility design while an N player remains outside the paid family.
2. The investors weakly prefer not to relinquish their sunk profits despite the pre-existing public interface; their **HOME GOVERNMENTS**, accounting for local consumer surplus, STRICTLY favor replacing the private design.
3. The NONINVESTING OUTSIDER government alone opposes public adoption because it must incur the mandatory fresh K; sunk founders do not pay K twice.
4. Under unanimity or sole-outside proposal rights, outsider opposition can preserve the private status quo. When at most two YES votes suffice and at least two favorable-capable governments can propose, this political shelter disappears, **and firms fully anticipate that at the investment stage**.
5. The rule change changes not only post-history votes but the **entire FIRST-STAGE pure-SPE investment correspondence**. Its world-welfare effects cannot be identified with one old investment optimum because old welfare support may straddle the public outcome.

**Do not market** simply as "two proposals are better than one" or "governments and private firms disagree." Those isolated propositions overlap earlier Harstad / Barrett–Yang / Takarada literature. The new possible result is the **ENDOGENOUS JOINT CAUSAL CHAIN** and its all-equilibria welfare implications.

## 2. Strongest formal propositions: two publishable results derived from one mechanism

### Result A (C1+C3+C7): complete equilibrium-selection mechanism

In the unchanged paid-family Cournot game, over all \((\eta,K)\in[.10,.14]\times[.54,.56]\), and all legal firm investment actions \(\{N,O(c,a):0\le a\le c\le1\}\), the private-terminal pure-SPE set is exactly \((O(c,a),O(c,a),N)\) up to firm relabeling, with
\[
R(\eta,K)=\{(c,a):0\le a\le c\le1,\ F_a(c,a;\eta)\ge0,\ F(c,a;\eta)\ge P,\ K>V(c,a;\eta)\},
\]
provided **unanimity \(r=3\)** or **sole-outsider agenda** with \(r\le2\). This is all profiles, not just symmetric interior candidates, and covers global one-firm deviations and all policy-triggered continuations (under the prescribed government decision rule).

Throughout R, founder **FIRMS** weakly favor L, founder **GOVERNMENTS** strictly favor M, and outsider government strictly favors L. With \(r\le2\) AND \(|A|\ge2\), **there is NO private-terminal pure SPE**; all public initial pure SPE are \(N\) or \(O(c_i,0)\) per firm.

**Novel economic interpretation:** preinvestment can purchase profitability but **not political veto rights** in one's own state; an outside national government protects the private standard via avoided future installation costs. Giving governments policy-access rights can completely extinguish private equilibrium investments without changing technological productivity or the final competition game.

**Why the proof carries weight:** global continuous investment-space iff, selection from all three-developer and asymmetric histories, exact constitutional cases. The strength is far above a three-vote counting identity. It is also conditional on a categorical third O(0,0) demand discontinuity and prescribed sincere government policy.

### Result B (C2+C5+C6): selection-free constitutional welfare frontier

C2: every **public pure SPE** has constant world welfare \(W_M=3(W_I-K)\), even though prepolicy initial vectors vary.

C5: world welfare among the FULL private SPE set achieves its unique maximum as a technology design at \((c,a)=(1,a^*(\eta))\), where \(F_a(1,a^*;\eta)=0\), and its value is \(W_R^{\max}=T(1,a^*)-2K\).

C6: for all old private-admitting constitutions and all new broad proposer constitutions \(r\le2,|A|\ge2\):
\[
\boxed{\left[\forall e_n\in E_{\rm new}^{pure}\ \forall e_o\in E_{\rm old}^{pure}\colon W(e_n)\ge W(e_o)\right]\iff K\le K_W(\eta)=3W_I-T(1,a^*(\eta)).}
\]

When \(K>K_W\), changing institutions **raises the lowest attainable equilibrium welfare and lowers the highest**. At \(\eta=.14,K=.55\), within the same original private-admitting constitution, valid pure equilibria O(1,0),O(1,0),N and O(1,.5),O(1,.5),N have private-minus-public world welfare of approximately \(-.106147\) and \(+.048788\), respectively. Hence ordinary adoption statistics or a selected example cannot rank the regimes robustly.

**Novel economic interpretation:** more uniform public standards do not necessarily dominate the *best privately adapted* standards, even though political reform removes low-welfare equilibria and reduces extreme equilibrium-selection risk. This is a **constitutional welfare-risk trade-off** between **robust worst-case improvement** and **best-case forgone private adaptation**.

**Avoid claims:** first welfare threshold in compatibility, first regional veto, first firm/state preference divergence, first weakest-link continuum; the mathematical sup-vs-constant comparison alone is standard. This result is novel, IF at all, because C1 and C5 ENDOGENOUSLY derive the old support, not because the final inequality is difficult.

A potential short pitch for later author editing:

> Private technology choices may determine not only which standards emerge but which governments have an incentive to protect them. We study a three-country environment where firms undertake sunk compatibility investments before a known public interface can be mandated through a national proposal-and-vote rule. We characterize the complete pure-strategy investment equilibrium correspondence.
