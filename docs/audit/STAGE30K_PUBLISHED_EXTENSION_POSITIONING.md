# Stage30K — How Published Theory Papers Sell Model Extensions: Primary-Source Comparison

Date: 2026-10-15 JST | Reassessment of PR67 Stage30J novelty re-kill; **no new economics or source claims, no math/model revisions**.

## Question and revised decision standard

Prior Stage30J conservatively treated a known component mechanism as close to zero originality even when its **endogenous interaction** with other decisions had not been previously analyzed. That confuses **novelty of individual primitives** with **novelty of equilibrium predictions derived jointly from a different game**. Published theoretical economics routinely uses known primitives and a new endogenous decision stage, policy institution, or sequencing to characterize previously unavailable equilibrium / welfare statements. This practice does **not** imply that any assemblage of old ingredients is new; it earns a contribution only if it changes a salient mechanism, yields a nontrivial theorem, has interpretable counterfactual policy consequences and is located precisely against the nearest earlier models.

## Six full or published-text theory comparisons

### A. Buccella, Fanti & Gori (2023), *Strategic product compatibility in network industries*, Journal of Economics 140, 141–168

**Primary:** final Springer published full HTML https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00712-023-00834-x, Introduction, §2–4 and Proposition 1. A deliberate Cournot extension of Katz–Shapiro (1985) and closely related Cournot standards/product-differentiation models. **Their own Introduction:** add a compatibility DECISION stage, separating endogenously or exogenously determined compatibility intensity; show quasi-fixed costs reshape SPNE multiplicity, introduce an anti-coordination/one-way-compatibility case, and change the relation between firm incentive and social welfare. Explicitly acknowledges a large installed body of network economics. The economic results—not novelty of Cournot, fixed costs or compatibility—sell the article.

**Our fair comparator:** Its multiple compatibility SPNE, cost thresholds, private/social welfare ranking make those **broad phenomena established**. Our model adds subsequent public mandatory policy with distinct governments and proposal eligibility, continuous two-stock matched private SPE *set*, outsider-specific K incidence and cross-constitution all-pure-SPE welfare. This is not mere "three vs two firms": different rational players can disagree with founders' profits. **Distinct possible discovery:** whose investment determines whose VETO, and how a state rule deletes the complete private equilibrium support. However no generic robustness is established to replacing prescribed policy selector or discontinuous three-O demand.

### B. Nabin, Sgro & Lahiri (2023), *The role of standard-setting organizations in deciding product quality and process innovation*, International Journal of Economic Theory 19, 767–786

**Primary:** published Wiley full HTML https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijet.12374, §2 taxonomy and explicit "Unlike these papers, we introduce several aspects together in a single framework" clause, §4.2–4.3 P2, P3, P4. Uses existing quality-process innovation theory with SSO *present versus absent*, product standard uncertainty and quality choices. Derives SSO minimum quality threshold for corporate profit, expansion of the technological gap and nuanced welfare comparative statics. It does NOT pretend to have invented quality, innovation, uncertainty or SSOs individually; the JOINT SETUP causes informative equilibrium changes.

**Our fair comparator:** Gives a strong precedent for integrated modelling as a contribution when it derives new predictions. The presence/absence of an SSO is exogenously compared, however, not an endogenous sequential government proposal/quorum game after companies choose private standard-family stocks. Our C1+C3+C6 are an exact *full equilibrium-support set* rather than the welfare effect of an exogenously assumed SSO; that is a potentially defensible difference. Do not imply the SSO itself must be state mandated.

### C. Farrell & Simcoe (2012), *Choosing the rules for consensus standardization*, RAND Journal of Economics 43, 235–252

**Primary:** published Wiley abstract https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1756-2171.2012.00164.x and author manuscript https://people.bu.edu/tsimcoe/documents/published/ConsensusRules.pdf (prior Stage30 checked multiple proposition numbers). Takes well-known bargaining without side payments / war of attrition and adds a *standard-setting design choice*, proposal screening, delay and an uninformed neutral mechanism. Key novelty **not that voting exists**, but that modifying rules influences ex-ante incentives to improve proposals, and information screening can conflict with delay.

**Our fair comparator:** Institution changing an *earlier* endogenous private decision is exactly the meaningful category of economic contribution, but not first in principle (Farrell–Simcoe/Harstad). Our different outcome is whether governments' proposers can eliminate **all** privately supportable prior investments and thereby change the welfare support (best/worst and universal comparison). It is not an informational war-of-attrition model and cannot claim their screening effects.

### D. Harstad (2005), *Majority Rules and Incentives*, Quarterly Journal of Economics 120, 1535–1568

**Primary:** final QJE publisher https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/120/4/1535/1926697 ; published abstract plus separate 2004 WP §5 Proposition 10 in prior Stage30 (WP number must not be ascribed to journal). Built on majority-vote/club policy and prepolicy investment; obtains sharply DIFFERENT investments as majority quorum changes (under- versus over-investment), and comparative-optimality results for diverse government sizes and preferences.

**Our fair comparator:** Established that voting rules change earlier investment; we cannot claim that abstract insight. But our switch condition involves *who may propose*, not just majority count, and founders' governments actually FAVOR overriding founders' investments while an outsider who did not invest vetoes. The promised economic tension is unique *supporting coalition identity and endogenous full private support*, if real and original within model. A two-proposer quorum tally alone is NOT a theorem-level original discovery.

### E. Costinot (2008), *A comparative institutional analysis of agreements on product standards*, Journal of International Economics 75, 197–213

**Primary:** published author PDF https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/standards_final.pdf ; PDF p.1 and p.4 visually inspected, §2.1–2.2. The comparison is *not* “new national treatment” or “new mutual recognition”; both have long existed. Its innovation is a fresh formally derived comparative institutional ranking with **opposite externality cutoffs** for imposing standards under NT and MR, and a welfare implication about the composition of traded goods. Importantly, p.4 says it focuses on Pareto-efficient, most-cooperative SPNE to do the institutional comparison rather than characterizing welfare against **every** possible initial equilibrium.

**Our fair comparator:** We can sell a different *institutional-policy diagnostic*: agenda change can eliminate the private equilibrium set but need not improve world welfare across all equilibria. The EXACT all-old-SPE quantifier is a genuine additional analytical burden over selecting cooperative equilibrium(s). This is not a claim to improve on Costinot's JIE contribution or share his WTO/EU institutional applicability.

### F. Takarada, Kawabata, Yanase & Kurata (2020), *Standards policy and international trade: Multilateralism versus regionalism*, Journal of Public Economic Theory 22, 1420–1441

**Primary:** final Wiley published text https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpet.12425, §§5–6 P5/P6, then §6.1 many/asymmetric firms, §6.3 fixed costs. Extends three-country mandatory standards/trade coordination to regional-vs-multilateral core stability. The CORE of their new result: welfare-maximizing global harmonization may be politically blocked by nonmembers when an externality parameter exceeds a cutoff, even if standards policies follow national treatment. The mathematical cutoff is important because it identifies which institutional configuration is sustainable. It also tests selected robustness with additional firms/costs.

**Our fair comparator:** Outsider blocking/welfare nonalignment alone is NOT new. Their decision makers are coalition governments choosing standards; ours are separate initial firm investors and latter national proposer holders, with opposite founder corporate vs home-government interests, unequal K incidence, and set-valued welfare over firm-investment pure SPE. The relative novelty is **who becomes protector after private investment** and which constitutional rule prunes equilibria, NOT "third country vetoes optimal harmonization" again. Like Takarada, numerical/cutoff economics should be connected to an understandable policy institution and distinguish social efficiency from stability.

### Supplementary: Bordignon & Brusco (2006), Journal of Public Economics 90, 2063–2090

**Primary:** journal published Introduction/abstract https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0047272706000235, prior Stage30D checked **2003 CESifo WP** P5–7 (2006 published proposition numbering remains inaccessible). Adds dynamic subunion status-quo and institutional choice to familiar federalism/cooperation, deriving when allowing early subgroup cooperation **may improve** later integration, not simply whether it raises cooperation today. Demonstrates strong "dynamic institution changes the feasible status quo" marketing; prevents generic first claims in our paper, while ours changes firms' continuous preinvestments and pure equilibrium support.

## Three diagnostic tests distilled from published extension practice

**T1. Opposite-to-obvious, conditional result:** Not merely “policy affects outcomes” but "policy changes first-stage investment and can eliminate self-sustaining private standards WITHOUT always raising welfare"; "commercial beneficiaries and political protectors are different actors". Published examples make similar distinction between familiar input mechanisms and unexpected equilibrium comparative statics.

**T2. Quantifier/solution concept is a substantive contribution:** Deriving the *entire* pure-SPE investment correspondence over a continuum with every strategic deviation, and establishing **for every old and every new equilibrium** welfare, is much stronger than reporting one optimizer or selected (cooperative) equilibrium IF the correspondence and welfare extrema are genuinely derived. It does not follow merely by asserting an abstract supremum identity.

**T3. Mechanism is externally interpretable and not tailored into the assumptions:** Published studies motivate rule rights, standard cost incidence and industry category structure. Here the fixed K-funded O(0,0) categorical switch and honest policy selector are imposed reduced-form restrictions. A successful paper MUST put them in the stated research question and limitations, not claim a universal standardization theorem. No field generality is established across fee g=.04 or strategic all-NO continuations.

## Conclusion on publication-level standards

**A combined model can legitimately yield ORIGINAL economic theory even when each ingredient has precedent.** The previous PR67 claim that only one “fully integrated” contribution is possible is appropriate *as an underlying mechanism count*, but making it sound like the study has NO plausible paper-quality results is too harsh. The integrated mechanism can support **two identifiable theorem-level contributions**: (1) the entire private/public investment SPE support as selected by national political eligibility, and (2) the selection-free constitutional world-welfare frontier and best/worst sacrifice. These are *two results*, not two unrelated primitive innovations.

**Preliminary economic-theory value: SUBSTANTIVE PUBLISHABLE CANDIDATE, conditional on accessible priority, honest restricted model and a precise referee-facing contrast.** This DOES NOT certify literature firstness, remove the Stage30J risk, validate empirical market status, or release manuscript/submission. Journal level should reflect economic mechanism and quality, not merely proof length.
