Date 2026-10-15 JST. Built on Stage30C baseline theorems and Stage30M Referee B. **Provisional passage proposals, not edits to manuscript .tex**. New formal claims are NOT introduced; all results are consequences of the original C1/C2/C3/C5/C6/C7, given their exact restricted baseline.

## 1. The institutional interaction is a 2×2, not an unconditional "two proposers" effect

Freeze two agenda sets *before investment*:
\(\mathcal A_s=\{3\}\) (single eligible proposer: country3);
\(\mathcal A_b=\{1,3\}\) (two eligible proposers, 1 and 3).

| Voting rule | A = {3}, only country3 may propose | A = {1,3}, countries1 and3 may propose |
|---|---|---|
| **r=3**, unanimity | Private-terminal pure SPE **exist** | Private-terminal pure SPE **exist** |
| **r=2**, majority | Private-terminal pure SPE **exist** only with **firm3 outsider** | **No** private-terminal pure SPE; all pure SPE end M |

These refer to **existence of private-terminal pure SPE**, NOT the absence of public equilibria in any of the first three cells. C2 guarantees an all-N public pure SPE in all constitutions. The identity of the N outsider in any private equilibrium is *determined at equilibrium*, and any fixed proposal agenda must refer to **country labels**, not to a movable "outsider" designation.

- **Along bottom row** r=2 fixed: \(\mathcal A_s\to\mathcal A_b\) eliminates private equilibria. This is a clean agenda-eligibility treatment.
- **Along right column** A={1,3} fixed: r=3→r=2 eliminates private equilibria. This is a clean quorum-reduction treatment.
- **Along top row** r=3 fixed: broadening A does **not** remove privately sustainable L because two YES never satisfy unanimity.
- **Along left column** A={3} fixed: lowering quorum from 3 to 2 does **not** remove the L pair whose outsider is country3, because the only eligible proposer votes NO.

**Strategic complementarity within the stated selector:** an eligible YES proposer and enough affirmative votes are jointly necessary. Neither "any agenda expansion" nor "any quorum reduction" suffices universally. The economics comes from which **nationality has invested** and hence bears a **new** mandatory K. There is no game-theoretically endogenous set of proposer identities after investments, and no generic claim that this institution describes an actual ISO, EU or WTO legal procedure.

## 2. Copy-editable main text: institutional comparison (draft, ~235 words)

*The role of agenda rights can be isolated from that of the approval threshold. Fix the two-vote requirement and consider two constitutions chosen before firms make their investments. Under the first, only country 3 is entitled to propose the public interface; under the second, countries 1 and 3 can propose it. In any privately sustainable investment profile of the first constitution, firms 1 and 2 develop the same private design and firm 3 remains outside. The corresponding national welfare gains from replacing the private design with the public interface have signs \((+,+,-)\): the two investing countries support replacement, but the noninvesting country's new qualification cost makes it oppose it. Because the sole eligible proposer is the outsider, the majority supporting replacement cannot initiate a mandate. Granting proposal rights to country 1 changes that outcome without changing the voting threshold. Indeed, our complete equilibrium correspondence implies that every privately sustainable investment profile is eliminated by this reform, and all pure equilibria in the second constitution end in public adoption. The comparison does not endogenously identify government agenda holders after investment; the two proposer sets are fixed country-specific constitutional rules. Nor does the result characterize unrefined strategic voting equilibria: government support and valid proposals follow the prescribed sincere national-welfare decision protocol.*

This is an illustration of C1/C3 rather than a new theorem. The old constitution has **public equilibria as well as private equilibria**. The comparative effect is the *disappearance of its private pure-SPE subset*.

## 3. Copy-editable main text: incidence explanation (draft, ~240 words)

*The political protection of a private standard does not originate with its developers' governments. An investing firm earns profit from its privately coordinated technology and weakly prefers its continuation to an immediate mandate. The same firm's government evaluates a different objective: the firm's worldwide profit plus consumer surplus in its own domestic market. As the firm has already incurred the basic open-family qualification cost, public adoption does not make it pay that entry expense again. In every privately sustained equilibrium of the benchmark, the country's consumer-surplus gain from replacing the private design is sufficiently large to overcome the firm's weaker private payoff; the founding country therefore strictly supports adoption. By contrast, the noninvesting outsider's firm has not paid the basic family qualification cost. A mandatory public interface requires that firm to incur the additional real setup expense, reducing its government's net gain from adoption. The outsider thus strictly opposes the mandate even though its own firms did not build the private standard. The resulting political protector is an uninvolved national government, not the firms' home governments and not an outsider firm vested with a legislative veto. This configuration is endogenously derived from the privately sustainable investment set and the national welfare accounts; the signs are not assigned to force a voting outcome.*

Technical caution: a founder's *corporate* opposition to M can be WEAK, not necessarily strict; it is false to imply founders' national governments change preferences between ex-ante and ex-post assessments. Formula appendix: corporate M−L \(=P-F-\phi a^2/2\le0\), founding national M−L \(=B>0\), outsider national M−L \(=V-K<0\). The latter applies within the C1 private-SP equilibrium set and stipulated selector, not to arbitrary off-path investments.

## 4. Copy-editable main text: credible welfare guarantee (draft, ~245 words)

*The comparative welfare consequence cannot be inferred solely from the public mandate's adoption. The old constitution supports a continuum of private investment equilibria as well as public equilibria. Public pure equilibria all deliver world welfare \(3(W_I-K)\), whereas the private equilibria have welfare \(T(c,a)-2K\), which depends on the endogenous design. We first characterize the complete private investment equilibrium support and prove that the highest world welfare among its designs occurs uniquely at \((1,a^*(\eta))\). The institutional comparison then holds for every possible pure equilibrium of both constitutions, without selecting a representative private design. Public-only reform weakly dominates all old pure equilibria if and only if \(K\le K_W(\eta)\). Above the cutoff, a reform can raise the lowest attainable equilibrium welfare but eliminate a private investment equilibrium whose welfare exceeds that of the public standard. The conclusion is stronger than comparing a single privately optimal-looking design with a public benchmark: the private design that enters the upper bound is proved to be a genuine equilibrium under the old institution, and a lower-welfare private equilibrium is simultaneously sustainable at the same parameters. This welfare-support comparison gives an ex ante guarantee under unspecified pure equilibrium selection, not an expected-welfare result, a first-best claim, or a recommendation that a particular real-world government enact the specified mandate.*

The universal comparison must include **old public pure SPEs**, and \(\inf/\sup\) (rather than assumed attained minima) should appear in displayed comparative results. Distinguish the post-sunk retrofitting cost \(\phi a^2\) from the new public-equilibrium ex-ante assessment.
