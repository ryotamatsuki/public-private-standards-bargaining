## B. Economic significance (not merely certified algebra)

**Mechanism in ordinary language.** A pair of firms cooperatively benefits from a private standard but each has unilateral incentives to reduce redundant compatibility investment. Their national governments, valuing consumers, want an already-available public standard, but the third country's government is hurt by fresh mandated setup expenditure K. It is the *uninvested outsider government*, not the founding governments, that blocks public replacement. The prospect of a mandatory fallback constrains viable private investments; expanding the number of governments allowed to propose can remove **the entire** remaining privately stable investment set. Since these private equilibria span multiple welfare levels, reform's effects on worst vs best equilibria can diverge. C1+C2+C5—not merely the inequality K≤KW—supply the nontrivial economics.

**Who acts:** firms invest N or O(c,a); governments support or block a fixed public M under an assumed sincere decision rule; firms comply; Cournot market clears. **Changes:** feasible first-stage pure equilibrium investment vectors, final L/M, national political gains and *world ex-ante* equilibrium welfare. **Importance:** rule changes can be powerful **without** guaranteeing an unconditionally welfare-improving equilibrium transition.

**Strongest predecessors:** Cornes & Hartley (2007) minimum-contribution continua; Harstad (2005) policies and initial investment/proposers; Bordignon & Brusco (2006) anticipatory three-country harmonization (final propositions unavailable); Takarada et al. (2020) outsider regional veto and fixed costs; Gandal & Shy (2001), Klimenko (2009), articles inaccessible final numbered theorem mapping. C1+C5+C6 **joint** result survives a bounded attempt but published priority not certified.

**Why the exact bound matters:** The condition \`K≤KW(eta)\` applies against **every old pure SPE**, not just a friendly selected private design. Above it reform strictly increases the equilibrium world-welfare infimum and decreases its supremum; opposite welfare-sign private pure SPE coexist at a fixed parameter. This is decision-relevant as a **set-valued risk assessment**, not as a realized causal effect. The numerical magnitude cannot be imported to real countries.

**Critical dependence:** fixed publicly available O(1,0), zero additional state implementation cost, cost reuse, N/O and pairwise min demand, a discontinuous third-entrant branch, prescribed policy selector. EU standards examples do not jointly instantiate these.

### Editor-facing pitch (155 words; conditional only)

A private compatibility standard can be protected by the country that did not develop it, even when its founding firms' governments strictly prefer public harmonization. We model firms' irreversible compatibility choices before a legally available public standard can be mandated under national proposal and approval rules. In a bounded three-country economy, we characterize all private pure-strategy investment equilibria rather than choosing the privately optimal standard as a representative outcome. Private coordination and the option to trigger public provision discipline investment; an uninvested outsider's fresh setup cost governs political protection. Giving more favorable governments proposal opportunities eliminates the entire private equilibrium set. We then compare the world-welfare sets generated by old and new constitutions. Public equilibria have identical welfare, whereas privately sustainable designs can be both above and below that outcome. We identify when reform is weakly better than **every** old pure equilibrium and when it improves the worst attainable welfare only by eliminating the best private equilibrium. This is a diagnostic benchmark, not an implementable treaty design.

Without a defensible categorical standard-membership interpretation and complete closest-published-theorem comparison, this is a specialized calibration rather than a general new standardization theory.
