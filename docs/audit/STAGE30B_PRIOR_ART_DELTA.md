## What can plausibly survive, and what cannot

The bare continuum and common-stock cost saving have high absorption risk.
The public wasted-stock accounting also is not a standalone general mechanism.
The potentially distinctive result is the **complete combination**: withdrawal
induces a public fallback; that fallback disciplines the privately sustained
minimum stock; a noninvestor's unincurred fixed cost filters the surviving set;
constitutional change deletes this set; public equilibrium welfare invariance
and the maximum over all private SPE provide a sharp welfare comparison
without an equilibrium selector.

No inspected parent theorem gives this whole game as an immediate corollary.
That is a limited search finding, **not confirmation of priority or publication
value**. The existence of close fallback/coordination and veto/selection papers
raises, rather than eliminates, the burden of proving substantive novelty.

The sharper welfare criterion also has a generic mathematical part: once a
constant public payoff and a maximum private payoff are known, universal
welfare dominance is equivalent to comparing those two numbers. Do not sell
this set-comparison tautology as the theorem's economics. The work and possible
economic insight lie in establishing the complete endogenous private set, the
absence of costly public equilibria, and the fact that its welfare maximum is
the canonical bilateral optimum.

Targeted searches covered weakest-link investment/fallback, standards
coordination, veto and irreversible partnership investment, and majority-rule
investment. They do not establish that all current literature has been searched.
Formal Stage6 re-kill remains required if the new full-paper architecture is
selected. Stop if the residual insight is just familiar coordination and costs
with a particular functional form; do not add another primitive to evade this
comparison.
