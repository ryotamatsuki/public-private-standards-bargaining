## 1. One-paragraph field-editor reason to publish (conditional argument)

A public interface can be supported by governments in the countries whose firms have already created a profitable private standard, while a nonparticipating foreign government protects that private standard to avoid fresh mandatory installation costs. This mismatch gives technology investments a political effect different from their private market return. We solve the complete endogenous continuous private-investment pure-equilibrium correspondence in such a sequential standardization game, including all policy-switching deviations, then characterize how proposal entitlements remove the whole privately sustainable set rather than merely changing an ex-post vote. Because a continuum of privately sustainable investments spans both sides of public-standard welfare, reform's success in establishing uniformity does not imply it improves world welfare. A theorem over the **entire** old/new pure-SPE welfare sets identifies exactly when expansion of agenda rights weakly improves welfare relative to every old equilibrium and when it improves worst-case welfare only by sacrificing the best private one. The contribution is conditional on a specific open-interface Cournot model and sincere governmental decision selector; it is not an optimal treaty or a general constitutional theorem.
